Activated carbon is a safe material when handled properly, but its fine particle size and high surface area create specific hazards. This guide covers essential safety protocols for industrial users.

## Primary Hazards

### 1. Combustible Dust
Activated carbon dust can form explosive mixtures with air:
- **Minimum Explosive Concentration**: ~50-100 g/m鲁
- **Ignition temperature**: 530-650掳C (lower than coal dust)
- **Kst value**: 100-200 bar路m/s (moderate explosion severity)

**Risk factors**:
- Dust generation during transfer, pneumatic conveying
- Accumulation on surfaces, inside equipment
- Static electricity buildup
- Welding/hot work near carbon

### 2. Spontaneous Combustion
Wet carbon or freshly activated carbon can self-heat:
- **Mechanism**: Adsorbed oxygen reacts exothermically with carbon surface
- **Trigger**: Moisture + organic vapors + poor heat dissipation
- **Signs**: Elevated temperature (>70掳C), CO production, sulfur smell

**Prevention**:
- Store dry (<5% moisture)
- Ensure adequate ventilation
- Monitor storage pile temperature
- Avoid large uncovered stockpiles

### 3. Inhalation Hazard (Nuisance Dust)
- Not acutely toxic but irritates respiratory tract
- Prolonged exposure can cause chronic bronchitis
- **OSHA PEL**: 15 mg/m鲁 (total dust), 5 mg/m鲁 (respirable fraction)

## Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

### Routine Handling
- **Dust mask**: N95 or P100 respirator
- **Safety glasses**: Sealed or goggles (prevents eye irritation)
- **Gloves**: Nitrile or leather (carbon is abrasive)
- **Coveralls**: Prevent carbon dust accumulation on skin/clothing

### Confined Space Entry
- **Supplied-air respirator** (SCBA or airline)
- **Atmospheric monitor**: O鈧? CO, LEL (Lower Explosive Limit)
- **Attendant** and rescue equipment

### Emergency Response (Fire/Spill)
- **Full-face respirator** with HEPA cartridges
- **Fire-retardant clothing**
- **Hard hat, steel-toe boots**

## Safe Storage Guidelines

### Warehouse Storage

**Bag/Super Sack Storage**
- Stack maximum 3-4 pallets high (collapse risk)
- Keep bags sealed (minimize dust, moisture pickup)
- Inspect for tears, leaks regularly
- Separate from oxidizers, flammable liquids

**Bulk Storage (Silos/Bins)**
- **Temperature monitoring**: Install thermocouples in center and periphery
- **Ventilation**: Passive venting to atmosphere (don't seal completely)
- **Inert blanketing**: Nitrogen purge for long-term storage (>6 months)
- **Level indicators**: Prevent overfilling and bridging

### Environmental Controls
- **Humidity**: Maintain <60% RH to prevent moisture pickup
- **Temperature**: Keep below 35掳C to reduce self-heating risk
- **Ventilation**: 6+ air changes per hour in bulk storage areas
- **No smoking/open flames**: Post signs, enforce strictly

## Material Transfer Best Practices

### Manual Handling (Bags)
- Use vacuum lifts or pallet jacks (bags are heavy: 10-25 kg)
- Cut bags over vessels to contain dust
- Wet-wipe spilled carbon before sweeping (reduce airborne dust)

### Pneumatic Transfer
- **Ground all equipment**: Prevent static discharge
- **Bonding jumpers**: Between transfer hoses and vessels
- **Explosion-proof motors/controls**
- **Slow ramp-up**: Minimize turbulence and dust generation
- **Baghouse/dust collector**: Capture fines at discharge points

### Conveyor Systems
- **Enclosed belts**: Reduce dust escape
- **Belt speed**: <1 m/s to minimize abrasion and dust
- **Clean regularly**: Remove spillage, prevent buildup

## Fire Prevention and Response

### Prevention Measures
1. **Eliminate ignition sources**:
   - No hot work within 50 ft of carbon storage
   - Explosion-proof electrical in classified areas
   - Ground and bond all transfer equipment
   
2. **Housekeeping**:
   - Daily cleanup of carbon dust
   - HEPA vacuums (never compressed air blowdown)
   - Minimize dust accumulation on beams, ledges

3. **Monitoring**:
   - Temperature alarms in storage silos
   - CO detectors (early warning of smoldering)
   - LEL monitors in enclosed transfer areas

### Fire Response

**If carbon ignites**:
- **Do NOT use water initially**: Wetting can accelerate combustion (oxygen evolution)
- **Smother**: Cover with inert material (sand, vermiculite) or CO鈧?- **Flood cooling**: After smothering, gradually wet with fine mist
- **Evacuate**: Carbon fires produce CO and toxic fumes

**Notify fire department**: Activated carbon fires are specialty response situations

## Disposal and Waste Management

### Spent Carbon Disposal

**Options**:
1. **Reactivation**: Thermal regeneration (preferred for large volumes)
2. **Incineration**: High-temp hazardous waste incinerator (1000掳C+)
3. **Secure landfill**: Non-hazardous waste (if no adsorbed contaminants)

**Regulations**:
- Test spent carbon for hazardous characteristics (TCLP for metals)
- Manifest as hazardous waste if contaminated with regulated substances
- PFAS-laden carbon requires special disposal

### Fines and Dust
- Collect via HEPA vacuum or baghouse
- Re-use if clean, or blend into bulk spent for reactivation
- Do NOT dump to sewer (will clog)

## Training Requirements

All personnel handling activated carbon should receive training on:
- **Hazard recognition**: Dust, self-heating, oxygen depletion
- **PPE use**: When and what to wear
- **Spill response**: Containment and cleanup procedures
- **Fire response**: Unique characteristics of carbon fires
- **Confined space**: Pre-entry testing, atmospheric monitoring

**Frequency**: Initial + annual refresher
