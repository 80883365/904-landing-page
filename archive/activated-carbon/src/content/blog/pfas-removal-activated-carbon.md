Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) are emerging contaminants with strict regulatory limits. Activated carbon remains one of the most practical technologies for PFAS removal at municipal and industrial scales. Here's what works.

## Why Activated Carbon for PFAS?

PFAS molecules have both hydrophobic (fluorinated tail) and hydrophilic (charged head) regions. Activated carbon adsorbs PFAS through:

1. **Hydrophobic interaction** with the fluorocarbon chain
2. **Electrostatic attraction** to charged surface groups
3. **Physical entrapment** in micropores

## Carbon Selection for PFAS

Not all activated carbons perform equally:

### Best Performers
- **Coconut-shell GAC** with high micropore volume (>0.45 cm鲁/g)
- **Coal-based GAC** with surface area >1100 m虏/g
- Virgin carbon (not reactivated) for maximum capacity

### Key Specs to Request
- Iodine number >1000 mg/g
- Micropore volume >0.40 cm鲁/g
- Low ash content (<5%) to minimize fouling

## Design Considerations

### Contact Time
- Minimum **10-minute EBCT** (Empty Bed Contact Time) for municipal drinking water
- **20-30 minute EBCT** for low-concentration industrial streams

### Breakthrough Prediction
PFAS breakthrough is compound-specific:
- **Short-chain PFAS** (C4-C6) break through faster
- **PFOA and PFOS** (C8) have longer run lengths
- Total PFAS may exceed regulatory limits while individual compounds remain below detection

### Pre-Treatment
- **Remove particulates** to prevent pore blockage
- **Control organics** (TOC) to reduce competitive adsorption
- Monitor pH (optimal 6-8 for most PFAS)

## Spent Carbon Disposal

PFAS-laden carbon requires **incineration** at high temperature (>1000掳C) or **secure landfill disposal** per local regulations. Standard reactivation may not destroy all PFAS compounds.

## Regulatory Context

- **EPA Health Advisory**: 4 ppt (parts per trillion) for PFOA and PFOS
- **State limits**: vary from 8 ppt to 70 ppt depending on jurisdiction
- Treatment must achieve **<2 ppt** to provide safety margin
