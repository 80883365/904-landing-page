Selecting activated carbon is rarely about finding the "best" carbon 鈥?it is about finding the **right** carbon for a specific job. The same high-iodine coconut carbon that excels in gold recovery may be over-specified and over-priced for a simple odor-control task. This guide walks through the key decisions.

## 1. Start with the phase: liquid or gas?

The single most important question is what you are treating.

- **Liquid-phase** (water, beverages, chemicals, oils) 鈫?granular (GAC) or powdered (PAC) carbon, typically coconut or coal.
- **Gas-phase** (air, vapors, exhaust) 鈫?pelletized or honeycomb carbon, chosen for low pressure drop and dust control.

## 2. Match the raw material to the pore structure

| Material | Pore structure | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coconut shell | Microporous | Small molecules, gold recovery, drinking water |
| Coal | Meso + microporous | Broad-spectrum industrial water, value applications |
| Wood | Mesoporous | Decolorization, large molecules |

## 3. Read the spec sheet correctly

Don't compare carbons by price alone. Look at the indicators that matter for your process:

- **Iodine number** 鈥?micropore capacity (small molecules). Aim for 鈮?1000 mg/g for demanding water/gold duty.
- **Methylene blue** 鈥?mesopore capacity (larger / colored molecules).
- **CTC activity** 鈥?gas-phase adsorption (carbon tetrachloride).
- **Hardness** 鈥?resistance to attrition; critical for re-use and reactivation.

## 4. Consider the whole life-cycle cost

A cheaper carbon that lasts half as long, generates more fines, or cannot be reactivated is rarely the better deal. Factor in **service life, reactivation potential, and disposal cost**.
