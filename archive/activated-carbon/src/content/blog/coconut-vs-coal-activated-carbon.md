Roughly 80% of the activated carbon sold globally comes from just two raw materials: **coconut shell** and **coal**. Both are excellent 鈥?for different reasons. Here is an honest, head-to-head comparison.

## Pore structure: the root of the difference

Coconut shell carbon activates into a **predominantly microporous** structure (pores < 2 nm). Coal carbon develops a **wider distribution** spanning micro- and mesopores. This single fact explains almost everything that follows.

## When to choose coconut

- **Gold recovery (CIP/CIL)** 鈥?micropores adsorb the aurocyanide complex efficiently.
- **Drinking water** 鈥?low ash and high purity.
- **Any application with small target molecules and a need for regeneration** 鈥?its hardness survives many reactivation cycles.

## When to choose coal

- **High-volume industrial water and wastewater** 鈥?broad-spectrum adsorption at the best cost-per-cubic-meter.
- **Solvent recovery and gas-phase work** 鈥?when paired with pelletizing.
- **Situations where the extra purity of coconut is not justified** by the application.

## A word on sustainability

Coconut shell is a **renewable agricultural by-product**, which gives coconut carbon a strong sustainability story. Coal is a finite resource 鈥?but spent coal carbon can often be **thermally reactivated** and reused multiple times, materially extending its lifecycle and reducing waste.

## Conclusion

There is no universal winner. The right choice is the one whose pore structure, purity and cost match **your specific stream and budget**. When in doubt, request samples of both and run a parallel bench test.
