But spent carbon is not necessarily waste. In many cases it can be **thermally reactivated** and returned to service, often at half the cost of new carbon.

## How reactivation works

Spent carbon is heated in a controlled, oxygen-limited environment (typically 800 鈥?1000 掳C). Adsorbed organics are volatilized and destroyed, while the carbon's pore structure is largely restored. After reactivation, the carbon typically regains **80 鈥?90% of its original adsorption capacity**.

## The economics

Compared to buying virgin carbon every cycle, reactivation typically delivers:

- **40 鈥?60% lower** net carbon cost per cubic meter treated.
- **Dramatically less** new raw-material extraction.
- **Reduced disposal** liability and hazardous-waste fees.

## When reactivation makes sense

Reactivation is most attractive for:

- **Large GAC users** with steady consumption (the logistics favor scale).
- **Carbons fouled by organic contaminants** (rather than inorganics or heavy metals, which reactivation does not remove).
- **Sites within economical freight distance** of a reactivation facility.

It is **less suitable** for powdered carbon, single-use impregnated grades, or streams heavily loaded with non-volatile inorganics.

## Custom vs. pool reactivation

Two service models exist:

- **Custom reactivation** 鈥?your carbon is kept separate and returned to you. Best when cross-contamination is a concern (e.g. food or pharma).
- **Pool reactivation** 鈥?spent carbons from many customers are blended, reactivated, and redistributed as a generic reactivated grade.
