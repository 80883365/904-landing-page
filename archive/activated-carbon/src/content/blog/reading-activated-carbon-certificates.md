A Certificate of Analysis (CoA) is your primary quality assurance document when purchasing activated carbon. But not all CoAs are equal鈥攁nd some suppliers provide incomplete or misleading data. Here's what to look for.

## Essential CoA Parameters

### Physical Properties

**Iodine Number (mg/g)**
- Indicates micropore development and surface area
- Higher = more adsorption capacity for small molecules
- **Typical ranges**: Coconut 900-1200, Coal 800-1100
- **Red flag**: Claims >1300 without supporting BET data

**Mesh Size Distribution**
- Must show % retained on each screen size
- Example: 8x30 mesh should be 90%+ between 8 and 30 mesh
- **Red flag**: Wide distribution (e.g., 50% fines below spec)

**Moisture Content (%)**
- As-packaged moisture by weight
- **Typical**: 2-5% for coconut GAC, 3-8% for coal
- **Red flag**: >10% (adds cost, indicates poor packaging)

**Ash Content (%)**
- Inorganic residue after combustion
- Lower = purer carbon, less fouling
- **Typical**: Coconut <5%, Coal <8%
- **Red flag**: Coal carbon sold as "coconut" with ash >6%

**Hardness/Abrasion Number (%)**
- Resistance to attrition and breakage
- Higher = less fines generation in use
- **Typical**: Coconut 95%+, Coal 90%+
- **Gold recovery spec**: >98%

### Chemical Properties

**pH (Aqueous Slurry)**
- Surface chemistry indicator
- **Typical range**: 6-10 for most applications
- **Drinking water**: 6.5-9.5 preferred
- **Red flag**: pH <5 (acidic leaching concerns)

**Bulk Density (g/L or kg/m鲁)**
- Weight per unit volume (loose packed)
- **Typical**: Coconut 450-500, Coal 420-480
- **Use**: Calculate vessel capacity and shipping cost

**CTC Activity (%)** _(for gas-phase carbon)_
- Carbon tetrachloride adsorption capacity
- Proxy for VOC removal performance
- **Typical**: Pellets 50-70%, Honeycomb 45-60%

### Specialty Tests (When Applicable)

**Methylene Blue Number**
- Measures mesopore volume
- Important for decolorization applications
- **Typical**: 180-250 mg/g

**Molasses Number**
- Large-molecule adsorption capacity
- Relevant for food/beverage, sugar refining
- **Typical**: 200-300 mg/g

**Heavy Metals (ppm)**
- Critical for drinking water, food, pharmaceutical
- **Limits**: Lead <5 ppm, Arsenic <3 ppm, Mercury <0.1 ppm

## CoA Red Flags

### 1. Missing Lot Numbers
- Every CoA must reference a specific production lot
- Generic or template CoAs without lot traceability are unacceptable

### 2. Unrealistic Specifications
- Iodine number >1200 for coal carbon (physically implausible)
- Hardness >99% (even best coconut rarely exceeds 98%)
- Ash content <1% for coal (achievable only with acid wash)

### 3. Incomplete Data
- Missing moisture or ash content
- No mesh size distribution
- Lacking test methods or standards referenced

### 4. Inconsistent Results
- CoA claims 1150 iodine but BET surface area only 900 m虏/g
- Bulk density outside typical range for carbon type
- pH values that don't match raw material source

### 5. No Test Dates
- When was the carbon produced and tested?
- Stale inventory (>12 months) may have degraded

## Interpreting Test Methods

Common standards referenced in CoAs:

| Test | Standard | What It Measures |
|------|----------|------------------|
| Iodine Number | ASTM D4607 | Micropore surface area |
| Mesh Size | ASTM D2862 | Particle size distribution |
| Moisture | ASTM D2867 | As-received water content |
| Ash | ASTM D2866 | Inorganic residue |
| Hardness | ASTM D3802 | Abrasion resistance |
| CTC | ASTM D3467 | VOC adsorption capacity |
| pH | ASTM D3838 | Surface acidity/alkalinity |

**European standards**: EN 12915 series (equivalent to ASTM)

**Chinese standards**: GB/T series (may differ slightly from ASTM)

## Sample CoA Review Checklist

Before accepting a shipment:

- [ ] Lot number matches purchase order and shipping docs
- [ ] All contracted specifications are met (not just "typical")
- [ ] Test dates within 90 days of shipment
- [ ] Supplier signature and QC stamp present
- [ ] Contact info provided for technical questions
- [ ] Third-party lab certificate (if specified in contract)
- [ ] Heavy metals tested (if required for application)

## Requesting a CoA

When soliciting quotes, specify:
- **Application** (water, air, gold, chemical)
- **Minimum specs** (e.g., iodine >1000, ash <5%)
- **Required test methods** (ASTM, EN, GB)
- **Frequency** (per lot, per shipment, quarterly)

**Pro tip**: Request CoAs from the supplier's last 3-5 shipments to assess batch-to-batch consistency.

## Handling Non-Conforming Material

If delivered carbon doesn't match the CoA:

1. **Stop use immediately** and quarantine the lot
2. **Third-party testing**: Send sample to independent lab
3. **Supplier notification**: Formal quality complaint with data
4. **Replacement or credit**: Per your supply agreement terms
5. **Root cause**: Was it mislabeling, bad production, or fraud?
