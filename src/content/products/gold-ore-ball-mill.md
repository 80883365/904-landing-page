## Gold ore ball milling engineered around the recovery circuit

A gold ore ball mill is the controlled size-reduction stage between crushing and mineral recovery. It uses a rotating cylindrical shell, liners and a selected charge of grinding media to reduce crushed ore to the liberation size required by gravity concentration, flotation or leaching.

The important purchase question is not simply “what diameter mill do you sell?” It is whether the complete grinding duty—feed size, ore competency, target P80, slurry density, circulating load and available power—can be kept stable day after day. Our proposal process starts with those inputs and then aligns mill type, discharge, liners, media and auxiliary equipment.

## Where the ball mill fits in a gold plant

A typical hard-rock route is **primary crushing → secondary crushing or screening → ball milling → classification → gravity, flotation or leaching**. The mill may operate in open circuit for a simple duty or in closed circuit with a spiral classifier or hydrocyclone when tighter product-size control is required.

Ball milling is usually the better fit than a small wet pan mill when the plant needs continuous operation, a finer product, a defined P80 or integration with a classification and pumping system. Wet grinding is common in gold processing because the slurry can move directly to classification and recovery while dust is reduced.

## What changes the final model selection?

- **Ore competency and abrasiveness:** hard, abrasive ore increases power demand and affects liner and media selection.
- **Feed size distribution:** a consistent feed protects throughput; oversized rock should be removed before the mill.
- **Target product size:** finer liberation may require a longer mill, closed-circuit classification or a regrind stage.
- **Wet or dry duty:** wet grinding needs water, drainage, pumps and slurry-compatible materials; dry grinding needs dust control.
- **Discharge arrangement:** overflow discharge supports controlled fine grinding, while grate discharge removes slurry faster for higher-throughput duties.
- **Downstream recovery:** gravity, flotation and leaching circuits each place different demands on particle-size distribution and slurry handling.

## Components that should be specified together

The shell, feed and discharge heads, liners, girth gear and pinion, bearings, drive motor, lubrication system and grinding media should be treated as one operating system. Rubber, manganese steel or composite liner choices affect lift profile, wear life and the energy transferred to the media. Forged steel balls are commonly used for demanding wet mineral grinding, with diameter and hardness matched to the mill and ore.

The supply scope can also include a feeder, trommel or screen, spiral classifier or hydrocyclone, slurry pump, control panel and the conveyors needed to connect the grinding stage to the recovery circuit. This is why a useful quotation needs process data rather than only a model number.

## Information to send for a firm quotation

1. Ore type, representative sample or Bond Work Index if available.
2. Maximum feed size and feed-size distribution after crushing.
3. Required dry-solids throughput in tonnes per hour.
4. Target product size or P80 and whether the circuit is open or closed.
5. Wet or dry operation, water availability and site climate.
6. Downstream recovery method, site voltage, frequency, altitude and installation constraints.

## Frequently asked questions

### What capacity can a gold ore ball mill handle?

Capacity varies widely with mill dimensions, ore hardness, feed size, product fineness and circulating load. The reference table on this page is for preliminary sizing; a final recommendation should be checked against representative ore data or a test-work report.

### Should I choose wet or dry grinding?

Wet grinding is often preferred when the next stage is classification, gravity concentration, flotation or leaching. Dry grinding can be appropriate when water is restricted or the required product must remain dry, but it requires a suitable dust-control and product-handling route.

### Should I choose overflow or grate discharge?

Overflow discharge is commonly used for controlled fine grinding. Grate discharge can remove slurry more quickly and suit higher-throughput duties. The correct choice depends on the target size, circulating load and downstream equipment—not on capacity alone.

### Which grinding balls should I use?

Forged steel balls are commonly selected for demanding wet mineral grinding because they balance hardness and impact toughness. Ball diameter, grade and top-up practice should be matched to mill size, ore characteristics and the required product size.

### Can the mill be supplied as part of a complete line?

Yes. A proposal can include feeding, crushing, classification, pumps, conveyors and downstream gravity equipment as required. Send the process route and throughput target so the interfaces are designed together.
